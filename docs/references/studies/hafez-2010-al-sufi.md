## Terminology

- **Old Arabic astronomical tradition / Arabic folk astronomy** are Hafez's labels for
  anwāʾ, lunar-mansion, weather, seasonal, and indigenous name material (§2.3,
  thesis pp. 31–39; §5.10, thesis pp. 313–319). They do not mean Najdi.
- **Ptolemaic constellation** and **al-Ṣūfī star number** belong to the scholarly
  catalogue layer. They must not be merged with folk-pattern membership.
- `بنات نعش الصغرى` — Hafez's table form *Banāt Naʿsh al-Ṣughrā*, provisionally
  “little daughters of the bier/coffin” (Table 18, thesis p. 314/PDF p. 327).
- `الجدي` — Hafez's *al-Juday*, glossed as kid/goat and crosswalked to Polaris
  (Tables 18 and 24, thesis pp. 314, 322/PDF pp. 327, 335). Kunitzsch's
  *al-gady/al-gudayy* discussion requires variant preservation.
- `بنات نعش الكبرى` — Hafez's *Banāt Naʿsh al-kubrā*, the Great Bear group
  (Table 19, thesis p. 314/PDF p. 327).
- `سرير بنات نعش` — *Sarīr Banāt Naʿsh*, provisionally “bed of the daughters of
  Naʿsh” (Table 19, thesis p. 314/PDF p. 327).
- *Dhāt al-Kursīy* is Hafez's transliteration for Cassiopeia in the translated
  catalogue table (PDF p. 151). Exact Arabic and translation are
  `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.

## Concepts and models

1. Al-Ṣūfī's work combines a Ptolemaic catalogue framework with reported Arabic
   star-name and asterism traditions (introduction, PDF pp. 14–18; §5.10,
   thesis pp. 313–319).
2. Hafez's translation/crosswalk is based on comparing manuscripts and printed
   editions; the thesis explicitly reports manuscript variation (§5.1, PDF
   pp. 250–261; §§5.11–5.12, thesis pp. 320–327).
3. Hafez's HR identifiers are modern scholarly crosswalks, not coordinates and not
   cultural approval. The thesis says the identifications use Toomer in Tables 24 and
   26 (thesis pp. 322, 324–325/PDF pp. 335, 337–338).
4. Historical pattern description, modern identification, and a modern educational
   route are distinct claims.

## Equations and algorithms

No Hafez equation is authorized for UFUQ production.

- The translated catalogue includes historical longitudes, latitudes, magnitudes, and
  a stated longitude addition in its headings, but these are historical scholarly data,
  not the approved numerical catalogue (Ursa Minor/Ursa Major tables, PDF
  pp. 129–141).
- Hafez recalculates/compares historical charts and supplies modern HR crosswalks in
  §§5.11–5.12 (thesis pp. 320–327). UFUQ must not copy those coordinates into the
  runtime catalogue.
- Required independent validation: resolve each HR proposal to an approved modern
  catalogue ID through a separate, versioned crosswalk; test pattern membership only
  after cultural review.

## Conventions

- Hafez's internal thesis page numbers are 13 less than local PDF pages in the studied
  Chapter 5 material (`thesis p. 314 = PDF p. 327`).
- HR numbers are Bright Star Catalogue identifiers, not UFUQ's automatically approved
  canonical key.
- Al-Ṣūfī star ordinals, Ptolemaic constellation membership, Arabic folk group
  membership, and IAU constellation boundaries are different conventions.
- Hafez's transliteration is retained as a source form; UFUQ has not approved a
  normalization system.
- The cited Ursa Minor translation begins from MS5306 folio 21 (PDF p. 124), but the
  broader translation uses multiple manuscript/edition witnesses. Every folio claim
  must be checked against §5.1 and the actual witness.

## Limitations

- A PhD thesis is secondary scholarship, not a primary Najdi source or production
  catalogue.
- Hafez's modern identifications require independent verification against a pinned
  catalogue.
- Manuscript/edition variants documented by Hafez prevent silent selection of one
  reading.
- The thesis does not approve UFUQ's Arabic, transliteration, cultural interpretation,
  pattern segments, or pedagogical route.
- No studied passage establishes specific Najdi geography.

## Conflicts and ambiguities

- Hafez's bier/coffin gloss conflicts with Kunitzsch's caution that this sense of
  `naʿsh` may be later folk etymology (Kunitzsch entry 55, printed p. 48).
- Hafez's *al-Juday* differs from Kunitzsch's historical reading discussion in entry
  107a (printed pp. 62–63).
- Table 19 supplies seven HR numbers, while Table 26 supplies star-by-star modern
  names. Those are related crosswalks, not independent cultural witnesses.
- The thesis sometimes uses broad “Arabic folk astronomy” language. UFUQ must not
  relabel it as Najdi.
