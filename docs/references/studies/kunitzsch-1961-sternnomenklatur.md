## Terminology

The following short source terms are retained because translation is consequential:

- `"arabische" Sternnamen` — “Arabic” star names in quotation marks. Kunitzsch warns
  that Arabic may identify the transmission language while the content is
  Hellenistic/ancient (Part I.A, printed p. 7; PDF p. 9).
- `echtarabisch` / `beduinisch-arabische Astrothesie` — categories used for material
  argued to be Arabic rather than translated Ptolemaic material (Part I.A–C, printed
  pp. 8–16). Exact English rendering is `HUMAN_REVIEW_REQUIRED`.
- `Ptolemäusübersetzungen` — Ptolemy-translation strata, distinguished from Arabic
  philological/anwāʾ strata (Part I.C, printed pp. 13–28).
- `echte ursprüngliche Sternnamen` — a limited proposed class of “genuine original”
  names (Part I.C.5 and D–E, printed pp. 20, 29–34). This is Kunitzsch's analytical
  category, not an automatic UFUQ classification.
- `banāt naʿsh`, `al-gady/al-gudayy`, and `al-kaff` are source transliterations whose
  exact vocalization/spelling must be checked against Arabic witnesses.

## Concepts and models

1. Star-name evidence is layered. Language, conceptual origin, source genre, and later
   astronomical identification must be separated (Part I.A–C, printed pp. 7–28).
2. Anwāʾ books and philological/lexicographical compilations transmit much of the
   material later described as Arab star nomenclature; al-Ṣūfī adds exact Ptolemaic
   identifications but also reports and critiques philological material (Part I.B–C,
   printed pp. 9–17).
3. A large list of names does not prove that every name was a widely used folk name.
   Kunitzsch distinguishes a smaller core from literary/philological constructions
   (Part I.C.4–5 and D–E, printed pp. 18–34).
4. Entry-level disagreements and corrupted readings matter. Entry 107a's treatment of
   the polar star distinguishes an older reading *al-gady* from the diminutive
   *al-gudayy* attested in later navigation material (printed pp. 62–63).
5. Similar words do not establish identical patterns. `kursī al-jawzāʾ` in entries
   148a–149 is an Orion-related “footstool” group, not evidence for Cassiopeia as
   *Dhāt al-Kursī* (printed p. 75).

## Equations and algorithms

No numerical algorithm from this source is suitable for UFUQ production.

- Entries use textual descriptions, source comparison, Ptolemaic star ordinals, Greek
  letters, and occasional manuscript/folio citations.
- Greek-letter identification strings in entries 55–56 are visibly OCR-degraded.
  Variables, coordinate frame, epoch, units, and uncertainty are not supplied in a
  form UFUQ can ingest.
- UFUQ use is therefore an evidence/conflict workflow: exact witness → philological
  classification → separately sourced modern catalogue identification → human review.
- Independent validation requires modern catalogue IDs from an approved crosswalk,
  never manually copied coordinates.

## Conventions

- Kunitzsch's printed-page numbers are used; local PDF pages are two greater in the
  studied body (`printed p. 48 = PDF p. 50`).
- Transliteration is the source's 1961 scholarly system, not an approved UFUQ system.
- Greek-letter identifications refer to modern constellation lettering in the author's
  analysis, while Ptolemaic ordinal references come through al-Ṣūfī. Neither is a
  coordinate authority.
- `al-kubrā/al-ṣughrā` classify the paired Banāt Naʿsh groups; they do not imply Najdi
  use.
- `al-gady`, `al-gudayy`, and Hafez's *al-Juday* must remain alternate readings until
  Arabic/philological review.

## Limitations

- OCR is unreliable for Arabic transliteration, Greek letters, diacritics, and some
  German words.
- The work analyzes transmitted literature; it does not by itself establish living
  practice in a particular modern region.
- No explicit Najdi evidence was found in the studied sections.
- Entry-level modern identifications predate current catalogue practices and need
  independent crosswalk evidence.
- German-to-English paraphrases here are provisional, not approved translations.

## Conflicts and ambiguities

- Entry 55 questions whether the “bier/coffin” sense of `naʿsh` is a later folk
  etymology. Hafez uses the bier/coffin gloss; UFUQ must record the disagreement.
- Entry 107a distinguishes older and diminutive forms for the polar name, while Hafez
  uses *al-Juday*. Do not silently standardize.
- Kunitzsch's source criticism warns against treating all material labelled “Arabic”
  as old Arabian or Bedouin.
- Cassiopeia-related *al-kaff* and Orion-related *kursī al-jawzāʾ* demonstrate why
  lexical resemblance is insufficient for pattern identity.
