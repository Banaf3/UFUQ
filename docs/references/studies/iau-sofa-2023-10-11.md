# Terminology

- **ICRS catalogue data**: catalogue right ascension and declination plus proper
  motion, parallax, and radial velocity, with an explicit catalogue epoch.
- **GCRS**: the geocentric celestial system used between barycentric/catalogue
  astrometry and Earth attitude.
- **CIRS**: the celestial intermediate system whose pole is the CIP and whose origin is
  the CIO.
- **Observed place**: in `iauAtco13`/`iauAtioq`, the direction a perfect geodetically
  aligned instrument would observe after Earth orientation, diurnal aberration, horizon
  rotation, and the selected refraction model.
- **CIP/CIO/TIO**: Celestial Intermediate Pole, Celestial Intermediate Origin, and
  Terrestrial Intermediate Origin.
- **Equation of the origins**: returned by `iauAtco13` as `ERA-GST`.
- **Quasi Julian Date for UTC**: SOFA's two-part representation whose civil day tracks
  UTC days of 86399, 86400, or 86401 SI seconds.
- **Two-part Julian Date**: a split representation used to preserve resolution; the
  routine preambles describe suitable JD, J2000, MJD, and date-plus-time splits.
- **Canonical model** versus **support function**: the preamble status distinguishes a
  defining model routine such as `iauEra00` from a convenience/composition routine such
  as `iauAtco13`.

# Concepts and models

## Astrometric chain

`iauApco13` prepares star-independent parameters for the chain
`ICRS <-> GCRS <-> CIRS <-> observed`. Its preamble assigns space motion, parallax,
light deflection, and aberration to ICRS/GCRS; frame bias and precession-nutation to
GCRS/CIRS; and Earth rotation, polar motion, diurnal aberration, horizon rotation, and
refraction to CIRS/observed. `iauAtco13` then calls `iauApco13`, `iauAtciq`, and
`iauAtioq` in that order.

`iauAtco13` expects ICRS right ascension and declination at J2000.0. Its Note 1 names a
Hipparcos catalogue epoch of J1991.25 as an example requiring preliminary propagation
with `iauPmsafe`. This is a routine-interface requirement, not approval of that routine
as UFUQ's production pipeline.

The lower-level composition exposes responsibilities hidden by `iauApco13`.
`iauEpv00` supplies Earth heliocentric and barycentric position/velocity and returns a
warning outside its documented 1900-2100 interval. `iauApco13` passes TT into
`iauEpv00` but discards that status, computes model CIP/CIO internally, assumes WGS 84
through its observer path, consults compiled leap-second state, and accepts refraction
inputs. `iauApco`/`iauApcs`, `iauGd2gce`, and the geometric portion of `iauAtioq` expose
the pieces needed for an explicit no-default route. `iauAtciq` applies solar light
deflection; `iauAtciqn` is the separate multiple-body extension. `iauApco` incorporates
the terrestrial observer position/velocity through `iauApcs` and explicitly sets the
later `astrom.diurab` term to zero because that local diurnal-aberration step would be
redundant.

## Proper motion

`iauPmsafe` propagates catalogue data assuming straight-line constant-speed motion in
the inertial frame. Its dates are two-part TDB Julian Dates. Its RA proper motion is the
coordinate rate `dRA/dt`, not the on-sky component multiplied by `cos(dec)`. It reports
separate warnings when distance is overridden, velocity is excessive, or its iterative
relativistic adjustment does not converge. A warning is evidence to preserve and
evaluate, not a successful silent fallback.

## Earth attitude

`iauPnm06a` forms a frame-bias/precession/nutation matrix using IAU 2006 precession and
IAU 2000A nutation, with TT as a two-part JD. `iauC2t06a` combines a
celestial-to-intermediate matrix, Earth Rotation Angle, the TIO locator, and polar
motion to transform GCRS vectors to ITRS vectors. It requires TT, UT1, and `xp`,`yp`
separately.

## Time scales

`iauUtctai`, `iauTaitt`, and `iauUtcut1` show that UTC, TAI, TT, and UT1 are distinct
inputs/representations. `iauUtcut1` makes the caller responsible for supplying the
`UT1-UTC` value matching the UTC. `iauUtctai` and `iauAtco13` can return a
`dubious year` warning and reject an unacceptable date. `iauDtf2d` is the named path
from calendar UTC to SOFA's two-part quasi JD.

## Observer and horizon

`iauAtco13` requires geodetic latitude, east-positive longitude, height above the WGS84
ellipsoid, and polar motion. It returns azimuth north-zero/east-90 and zenith distance.
`iauHd2ae` supplies a purely rotational HA/Dec-to-horizon transform and explicitly
states that its azimuth is relative to the rotational north pole; sub-arcsecond work may
need polar-motion and deflection-of-the-vertical treatment. Neither routine supplies a
terrain horizon.

## Atmospheric refraction

`iauRefco` supplies the coefficients of the compact `tan(z)` model used by
`iauAtioq`. It depends on pressure, temperature, relative humidity, and wavelength.
The preamble documents omitted atmospheric/geophysical effects and test ranges.
`iauAtioq` guards the numerical model at low altitude; that guard is not a statement
that near-horizon refraction is physically accurate.

# Conventions

- Angles in the studied astrometry and horizon routines are radians unless a parameter
  explicitly uses arcseconds, degrees Celsius, hPa, kilometres per second, or
  micrometres.
- Longitude is east-positive in `iauAtco13`/`iauApco13`.
- Observer latitude is geodetic and height is above the WGS84 ellipsoid for the `13`
  observed-place convenience path.
- Observed azimuth is north `0`, east `pi/2`; zenith distance, rather than altitude, is
  returned by `iauAtco13`/`iauAtioq`.
- `iauHd2ae` normalizes azimuth to `[0,2pi)` and altitude to
  `[-pi/2,+pi/2]`; `iauAe2hd` returns HA in `[-pi,+pi]`.
- UTC uses a quasi-JD convention across leap seconds; TT, TAI, UT1, and TDB use named
  two-part Julian Dates according to each routine contract.
- `iauPmsafe` expects RA proper motion as coordinate-angle rate and dates as TDB.
- Polar-motion coordinates are radians and are not interchangeable with longitude or
  latitude corrections.
- Refraction-free operation is achieved through zero refraction coefficients/inputs,
  but the UFUQ policy must explicitly define how this is requested and labelled.

# Testing implications

- Unit tests: angle normalization; HA/Dec-horizon cardinal cases; two-part-JD split
  equivalence; status propagation; geodetic input validation before calling permissive
  SOFA-like routines; and zero-pressure geometric cases.
- Independent reference fixtures: catalogue epoch, observation UTC, UT1, TT, `xp`,`yp`,
  observer datum/height, meteorology, wavelength, space-motion fields, routine/version,
  and output frame/convention must all be present.
- Numerical comparisons: compare great-circle separation and wrapped azimuth; also
  report component residuals, status/warning mismatch, and geometric versus refracted
  output separately.
- Boundary partitions: reference epoch; nonzero/high proper motion; zero or negative
  parallax; UTC day and leap-second boundaries; nonzero polar motion; meridian,
  east/west, horizon, zenith/nadir, and invalid dates.
- Invariants: `iauHd2ae`/`iauAe2hd` round trip away from singularities; normalized
  vectors retain unit length; alternate valid JD splits agree within measured numeric
  resolution.
- Stop conditions: missing required frame/epoch/time scale, source-row parallax/RV,
  approved leap/EOP field state, observer datum/height, or activated supported domain
  blocks V1 execution. Missing production/reference residuals or numerical tolerance
  blocks scientific acceptance after implementation, not implementation entry.
- Oracle independence: the Python/Astropy producer must not import UFUQ production
  packages or mechanically translate the selected TypeScript algorithm.

# Limitations

- SOFA does not select UFUQ's catalogue, observer, supported dates, atmospheric state,
  horizon, visibility rule, approximation mode, or tolerance.
- `iauAtco13` is a composed convenience path with fixed contemporary SOFA models. Its
  presence did not select production; the approved lower-level mapping exists to keep
  ellipsoid, leap/EOP, refraction and status ownership explicit.
- `iauHd2ae` is only a coordinate rotation and does not turn catalogue coordinates into
  apparent/topocentric coordinates.
- Refraction accuracy statements are conditional on the model and inputs; they do not
  validate assessment behavior near the horizon.
- SOFA's internal testbed verifies a SOFA build against SOFA expected values; it is not
  independent validation of UFUQ.
- The studied routines do not establish I/311 field semantics, data quality, or
  redistribution rights.

# Conflicts and ambiguities

- The I/311 `ReadMe` alone does not expand `pmRA`, but official I/311 Appendix G Table
  G.3 defines it as `mu_alpha_star`. `iauPmsafe` requires `dRA/dt`; direct field reuse is
  therefore prohibited, and the conversion is singular at the celestial poles. The
  CDS-defined 365.25-day `yr` supports the numeric SOFA Julian-year rate conversion,
  but the I/311 derivative time scale remains unresolved.
- *Fundamental Astronomy* uses a south-origin astronomical azimuth in its explanatory
  chapter, whereas SOFA and UFUQ use north-zero/east-positive azimuth. UFUQ must retain
  the explicit project/SOFA convention.
- SOFA's ability to set `xp`,`yp` to zero “for many applications” is not evidence that
  UFUQ may do so. An omission requires a bounded, approved scope.
- The SOFA preambles reference earlier IERS Conventions in some routine notes. UFUQ's
  registered baseline is TN36 (2010), with later IERS corrections tracked separately.
- SOFA routine warning ranges and model-accuracy figures are not equivalent to a
  supported UFUQ date range or acceptance tolerance.
